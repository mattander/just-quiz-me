# Quiz Quality Rules

## Research Notes

- Multiple-choice guidance from NC State DELTA and University of Michigan says strong stems should be clear, focused, and concise. Answer choices should be plausible, homogeneous, mutually exclusive, and similar in length/detail.
- University of Michigan also recommends that a stem stand alone as a meaningful question and that options avoid unnecessary reading burden.
- Digital.gov's plain-language guidance emphasizes writing that is clear and easy to understand for the intended audience.
- Nielsen Norman Group's web-writing research emphasizes concise, scannable, objective language and warns that users dislike fluffy or overhyped prose.

Sources:

- NC State DELTA: https://teaching-resources.delta.ncsu.edu/multiplechoice/
- University of Michigan LSA: https://lsa.umich.edu/technology-services/news-events/all-news/teaching-tip-of-the-week/a-refresh-on-writing-quality-multiple-choice-questions.html
- Digital.gov Plain Language: https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language
- Nielsen Norman Group: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/

## Just Quiz Me Translation

This is not an exam, but the craft rule still applies: options must be good choices before they are good jokes.

### Strong Prompt

- Names a specific situation.
- Asks one thing.
- Uses everyday wording.
- Gives enough context for the answers to make sense.

Example:

`A friend borrows your favorite hoodie and returns it late. What bothers you most?`

### Strong Options

- Each option directly answers the prompt.
- Each option describes a decision, reaction, preference, fear, or habit.
- All options live in the same category.
- The funny detail clarifies the behavior instead of replacing it.

Example:

- `They did not warn you it would be late.`
- `They returned it clean but stretched out.`
- `They acted like borrowing it gave them partial ownership.`

### Weak Options

- Require the reader to decode a metaphor.
- Use a punchline instead of a behavior.
- Depend on internet tone rather than observation.
- Could belong to any quiz after swapping nouns.

Example:

- `They refuse to explain the sky.`
- `They have the confidence of a lawsuit.`
- `The vibes are ancient and damp.`

## Tone Rules

- Keep the voice dry, specific, and conversational.
- Let absurd nouns stay absurd; make the human behavior normal.
- Prefer "the thing a person actually does" over "the weirdest way to say it."
- Avoid slang as a shortcut for personality.
- Use mild accusation. Do not dunk on the reader.

## Result Mapping Rules

Every result needs a short internal brief before drafting questions:

- Result A: what pattern does this represent?
- Result B: what pattern does this represent?
- Result C: what pattern does this represent?

Then every option should map to one brief. If it maps only to a noun, the quiz is not ready.

## Review Rubric

Score each question from 0 to 2:

- 0: Prompt/options are incoherent, mismatched, or mostly joke fog.
- 1: Prompt is understandable, but at least one option is vague, overly zany, or weakly mapped.
- 2: Prompt is clear; options are comparable, plausible, and mapped to distinct result identities.

Rewrite any question below 2 before publishing.
